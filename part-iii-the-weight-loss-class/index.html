Part III

The Weight-Loss Class

This is where the evidence is strongest, by a wide margin. It is also the only category in this guide where multiple compounds have been tested in tens of thousands of people and demonstrated benefits on outcomes that matter, not just on biomarkers.

These drugs mimic incretins — gut hormones released after eating that signal fullness, slow stomach emptying and improve insulin response. Understanding them well is worth the effort, because they set the standard against which everything else in this book should be measured.

Semaglutide

APPROVED Sold as Ozempic (type 2 diabetes), Wegovy (weight management) and Rybelsus (oral, diabetes), from Novo Nordisk. A GLP-1 receptor agonist.

What it does. Semaglutide binds the GLP-1 receptor, reducing appetite and food intake, slowing gastric emptying and improving glucose-dependent insulin secretion. Structural modifications protect it from enzymatic breakdown and let it bind albumin in the blood, producing a half-life long enough for weekly dosing.

What the trials showed. In STEP 1, a 68-week randomised trial of 1,961 adults with overweight or obesity without diabetes, once-weekly semaglutide 2.4 mg produced a mean weight reduction of 14.9% compared with 2.4% on placebo [1]. The cardiovascular question was answered separately by SELECT, which enrolled over 17,000 adults with cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity but without diabetes: semaglutide reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by about 20% relative to placebo over a mean of roughly 40 months [2]. That second result matters enormously — it moved semaglutide from a drug that changes a number on a scale to one shown to prevent heart attacks and strokes.

An oral formulation for weight management was subsequently approved in December 2025.

Approved dosing. For weight management, escalated over approximately 16 weeks from 0.25 mg to 2.4 mg once weekly. Prescription only, under medical supervision.

Known harms. Gastrointestinal effects dominate — nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation — typically worst during escalation. Less common but serious risks include pancreatitis and gallbladder disease. It carries a boxed warning regarding thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents and is contraindicated in people with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2. Weight is substantially regained after discontinuation, which is why it's framed as long-term therapy rather than a course of treatment.

Tirzepatide

APPROVED Sold as Mounjaro (diabetes) and Zepbound (weight management, obstructive sleep apnoea) from Eli Lilly. A dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist.

What it does. Tirzepatide activates two incretin receptors rather than one. The contribution of the GIP arm is still debated mechanistically, but the clinical effect is consistently larger than GLP-1 alone.

What the trials showed. In SURMOUNT-1, 2,539 adults with obesity or overweight received tirzepatide or placebo for 72 weeks. Mean weight reduction reached approximately 21% at the higher doses, with placebo at about 3% [3]. In head-to-head comparison against semaglutide, tirzepatide produced greater weight reduction. It is also approved for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea in adults with obesity.

Approved dosing. Started at 2.5 mg weekly and escalated in 2.5 mg steps at four-week intervals to a maintenance dose of 5, 10 or 15 mg once weekly. Prescription only.

Known harms. Essentially the same profile as semaglutide: predominantly gastrointestinal, worst during escalation, with the same boxed warning for thyroid C-cell tumours and the same contraindications. Weight regain after stopping is likewise expected.

Retatrutide

CLINICAL — investigational, not approved anywhere. A once-weekly triple agonist at the GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptors, from Eli Lilly.

What it does. Retatrutide adds glucagon receptor activity to the dual-agonist template. Glucagon receptor agonism is thought to increase energy expenditure and reduce hepatic fat, complementing the appetite suppression of the incretin arms.

What the trials showed. The Phase 2 trial published in 2023 reported mean weight reduction of 24.2% at 48 weeks on the highest dose, with the weight curve not yet plateauing [4]. Phase 3 results have since arrived. In TRIUMPH-4, in adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis, retatrutide reduced weight by up to an average of 28.7% and reduced pain by up to 75.8% [27]. In TRIUMPH-1, reported in May 2026 across 2,339 participants, mean weight reduction at 80 weeks was 17.6% on 4 mg, 23.7% on 9 mg and 25.0% on 12 mg, against 3.9% on placebo; a subgroup escalated to maximum tolerated dose reached up to 30% reduction at 104 weeks [28].

Dosing. No approved dose exists. Trials escalated to maintenance doses of 4, 9 or 12 mg weekly.

Known harms. Gastrointestinal effects at higher rates than the dual agonists, along with increases in heart rate. Because glucagon receptor agonism affects hepatic glucose output, glycaemic and liver parameters were monitored closely in trials.

The honest frame. These are the largest weight-loss figures ever reported in Phase 3 obesity trials. They are also results from a compound that no regulator has yet reviewed and approved. Material sold online as "retatrutide" has no connection to Lilly's manufacturing, and none of the safety monitoring that accompanied these trials applies to it.

CagriSema

CLINICAL — investigational, under FDA review. A fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide, a long-acting amylin analogue, with semaglutide, from Novo Nordisk.

What it does. Amylin is a satiety hormone co-secreted with insulin that signals fullness through a pathway distinct from GLP-1. Combining the two targets appetite from two directions.

What the trials showed. In REDEFINE 1, participants who adhered to treatment lost an average of 22.7% of body weight over 68 weeks [29]. In adults with type 2 diabetes (REDEFINE 2), the reduction was smaller, as is typical for this class in diabetic populations. Worth knowing for balance: a head-to-head trial against tirzepatide reported in 2026 did not demonstrate non-inferiority, which tempered expectations that the combination would displace the leading dual agonist.

Novo Nordisk filed a New Drug Application with the FDA for CagriSema in adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related comorbidity [30]. As of early 2026 it was not FDA approved, with review expected during 2026 [30].

Dosing. Cagrilintide 2.4 mg with semaglutide 2.4 mg, once weekly, in trials. No approved dose.

Known harms. Gastrointestinal effects consistent with the incretin class, plus injection-site reactions.

Survodutide and mazdutide

CLINICAL Two further dual agonists worth knowing by name.

Survodutide (Boehringer Ingelheim) is a GLP-1 and glucagon receptor dual agonist. Phase 3 data reported in 2026 showed mean weight reduction of roughly 16.6% at 76 weeks, and Phase 2 results in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis were encouraging. Not FDA approved.

Mazdutide (Innovent) is also a GLP-1 and glucagon dual agonist, and became the first drug of its class approved for weight management anywhere when it was cleared in China in 2025. Trial data showed weight reductions in the mid-teens as a percentage, with notable reductions in liver fat. It is not FDA approved, and most of its evidence base comes from Chinese populations.

Orforglipron — and why it belongs in a peptide guide

APPROVED Approved by the FDA on April 1, 2026 as Foundayo, the first oral non-peptide GLP-1 receptor agonist; in the ATTAIN-1 Phase 3 trial it produced 12.4% weight loss at 72 weeks [31].

It's included here for an instructive reason: orforglipron is not a peptide. It is a small molecule that happens to activate the same receptor. Because it isn't a peptide, it survives digestion and can be taken as a daily pill without the timing restrictions oral semaglutide requires.

The lesson generalises. What matters therapeutically is the receptor and the evidence, not whether a molecule falls into the chemical category "peptide." The category is not itself a virtue, and marketing that treats "it's a peptide" as a selling point is trading on a halo rather than an argument.

AOD-9604 and HGH Fragment 176-191

PRECLINICAL for the promoted use, with a negative human trial. These are fragments of the growth hormone molecule marketed for fat loss.

The rationale was appealing: isolate the lipolytic portion of growth hormone while leaving behind its effects on growth and glucose metabolism. It is a genuinely elegant idea. It was tested, and in a large Phase 2b obesity trial AOD-9604 did not separate from placebo on weight loss. Development for obesity was discontinued; later work explored cartilage and joint applications.

HGH Fragment 176-191 is closely related and is frequently marketed using AOD-9604's mechanism story — while quietly omitting that the mechanism story ends in a failed trial.

This is one of the clearest cases in the guide where an attractive hypothesis was properly tested and did not hold up. Marketing in this category continues largely unchanged.
